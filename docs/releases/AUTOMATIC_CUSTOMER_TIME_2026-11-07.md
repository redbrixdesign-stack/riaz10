# Automatic customer time — 7 November 2026

Release candidate: deployment identity and public acceptance are appended after promotion. This is pilot-stage, location-supported operational memory, not certified billing/attendance evidence or native background tracking.

## Implemented behavior

- Navigate starts one customer-linked journey. Repeated taps share one start; another target cannot inherit the pending/active trip. Closed visits and an already-running on-site session do not start another tracked journey; Maps can still open.
- Two fresh, accurate readings near the resolved customer location, at least 30 seconds apart, confirm arrival. Arrival is recorded at the first qualifying reading. Driving past, poor accuracy, stale/out-of-order fixes and postcode-only lookup results do not establish arrival. A foreground confirmation check supports a stationary arrival.
- Arrival ends travel timing and automatically arms on-site departure detection. Departure needs two reliable readings outside the arrival area, at least 30 seconds apart; it records the first outside observation and its preceding inside boundary. The current pilot thresholds are accuracy ≤50m, arrival distance plus accuracy ≤150m, stable arrival movement ≤50m, reported arrival speed ≤2.5m/s, and departure distance minus accuracy >250m. These are heuristics requiring field validation.
- Customer time is travel to that customer plus on-site elapsed time. Earlier on-site sessions for the same visit are retained and included. Missing legacy travel time is shown as incomplete, not zero; explicitly reviewed unknown travel does not revert to an old estimate.
- Physical departure does not complete a visit or change earnings. Recording a work outcome does not end physical on-site time. A completed-but-genuinely-tracked on-site customer stays visible on Home with View timer. After departure, Home moves to the next unvisited customer while retaining unfinished outcome work. Home updates retain the existing search/control nodes.
- Customer workspace and Visit show travel, on-site and total time. Manual arrival/end and Resume detection are fallbacks; Review times records UK arrival/departure and a travel-time override with retained correction history. It neither rewrites trip mileage nor changes the visit outcome/payment. A stale review or failed write remains an explicit error.

## Reliability, privacy and boundaries

- Detection runs only for an adviser-started journey/on-site session. Temporary on-site anchors and detector recovery are encrypted on this device, with generation guards against late writes after a stop. Session identity and arrival timestamps guard stale callbacks; ending/reviewing time is serialized and transaction-backed where supported. Cancelled/changed records stop the on-site watcher; a 12-hour guard ends prolonged detector operation and flags review.
- Raw on-site GPS samples are not sent to map/AI providers. Navigation geocoding/routing retain their existing provider boundaries. The in-app privacy explanation now describes visit timing. No API keys, server settings, financial calculations or autonomous sending were added.
- Browser location delivery can pause while Beelo is hidden or the phone is locked. Reopen Beelo after using Maps. Gaps, failed saves and inferred closure on the next journey are flagged as estimates for review; the app does not invent an exact unseen arrival/departure. GPS detection cannot prove property boundaries, distinguish a nearby prolonged traffic stop perfectly, or certify continuous attendance. See the [W3C visible-document geolocation rules](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/#request-a-position).
- Continuous dependable tracking while Maps is foregrounded or the phone is locked remains a native-app requirement, **not implemented**. Browser routing/mileage may also miss observations during suspension. Real iPhone/Safari/installed-PWA, battery/permission and field-threshold acceptance remain open; simulated signals are not physical-device evidence. A pending start can be lost if the browser is terminated before recovery storage commits.

## Validation

A 41-journey synthetic browser sweep passed on the first integrated timing build. After final boundary/concurrency hardening, eight affected journeys passed on the release candidate in Chromium/WebKit: automatic customer time, Home, active-visit selection, customer workspace, navigation review, contact handoff, cold offline startup and service-worker update. The final complete unit suite and targeted timing/Home/documentation checks passed; the timing journey was repeated after the final narrow-phone wording adjustment.

The new browser journey uses real encrypted storage with **controlled synthetic GPS** and real navigation/outcome/review UI. It checks drive-by rejection, arrival/departure debounce, on-site jitter, travel + site total, unfinished fitting retention, Home continuity, completed £20 service-call timing, encrypted reload, offline gap handling, failed review writes, explicit correction and unchanged mileage. Unit/storage tests also cover failed departure saves, stale review rejection, trip retry/idempotency, explicit unknown travel, double navigation, stale encryption/confirmation callbacks, cancelled/long-running sessions and stored-timestamp attribute safety. Layout/accessibility checks retain the existing Home range of 320–1280px; time review is checked at 320px. No real calls, messages, provider requests or social sharing were delivered by these tests.

One targeted rerun against the old Python loopback fixture captured connection-reset/socket failures for startup scripts. The final eight checks used a Node HTTP fixture like the passing full release runner. No production startup fix is claimed from this fixture adjustment.
